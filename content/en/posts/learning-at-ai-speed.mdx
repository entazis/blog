Since working with AI agents, I’ve started to question something simple:

**What is my actual learning speed now?**

Not how fast I can ship.
Not how fast code is generated.
But how fast I can *understand what just happened*.

Because those are no longer the same thing.

---

AI has changed how I learn in two distinct modes:

## 1. Autopilot mode

I let the agent execute tasks with a known trajectory.
I define the goal, it fills in the implementation.

Fast. Efficient. Minimal friction.

But learning here is… incidental.

## 2. Shadowing mode

I follow the agent’s thinking step by step.
I question decisions, explore alternatives, inspect mistakes.

This is slower, but this is where actual learning happens.
This is where blind spots surface.

---

The problem is: these two modes don’t scale together.

Because while output speed increases linearly…
**understanding doesn’t.**

---

This creates something I’ve started to feel more and more:

**Comprehension debt.**

It’s the gap between:

- what your system does
- and what you can confidently explain

You can ignore it. For a while.

But it compounds.

---

So the real question becomes:

**How much comprehension debt can you handle?**

- When do you stop generating and start understanding?
- Is maintaining good documentation enough?
- Can you rely on the agent to “own” the complexity?

Or are you just postponing a future debugging nightmare?

---

There’s also a surprising twist.

Sometimes I see the agent going in the *wrong direction*.
I’m about to step in, correct it, guide it.

Then I wait.

And it fixes itself.

---

Other times, I spot something in the code that looks wrong.
I dig into it… and realize:

**It’s not wrong. It’s better than what I would’ve written.**

That moment is different.

That’s not just acceleration.

That’s **learning beyond your current level**.

---

So now I think about development differently:

- How fast can I build?
- How fast can I learn?
- And most importantly:

**How fast can I *understand* what I’ve built?**

Because that might be the real bottleneck now.
